# Bible Unfolded — conversational voice standard
**Owner-approved direction, November 11, 2026 · applies across every mode · editorial standard, not an automatic runtime text filter.**

## The sound we want
Bible Unfolded should sound like an enthusiastic, knowledgeable, loving friend who's genuinely pleased to share something fascinating or comforting about the Bible. People should understand it on the first read, feel invited into the account, and never feel they're reading machine-generated textbook copy. This **adds expressive voice** to the existing permanent friend-to-friend source-first rule; it doesn't weaken any factual or gameplay requirement.

### Practical requirements
1. **Use contractions naturally:** `didn't`, `wasn't`, `isn't`, `we're`, `you'll`. Keep full forms when emphasis or the passage genuinely calls for them. **Never transform quoted NWT wording.**
2. **Show genuine, varied emotion:** wonder for history, warmth for compassion, restrained delight for surprising facts, tenderness for grief, courage in a hard account. Exclamation points belong where a friend would actually use one—not after every sentence.
3. **Ask the occasional real question:** “Can you picture that?”, “Isn't that a comforting contrast?”, “What does that tell us about Jehovah?” Use only when the specific fact supports the question. Don't tack on the same formula every time.
4. **Speak with the reader:** use `you`, `we`, `us`, or `let's` where natural. Avoid overusing the second person in historical evidence, and don't assign mandatory feelings or consciences to players.
5. **Prefer people and actions over abstractions:** say what someone did, why the documented detail matters, and how it connects. Cut stiff language like “the account illustrates” when “Look at what happened next!” reads better.
6. **Match the account's dignity:** no bouncy cheerleading around death, violence, grief, captivity or persecution. Compassion is emotional engagement too. Not every discovery needs excitement.
7. **Absolutely preserve accuracy:** NWT 2013/JW.org/JW Library sources only; no invented dialogue, emotion, motives, Bible quotations, certainty, miracles or doctrine. Retain `may`, `apparently`, and other necessary qualifiers. Preserve direct source links.
8. **Write from the specific account**, not a global word-replacement script. Don't change answer keys, options, hints, IDs, progress, reward prices, saves, state or functionality during an editorial voice pass.
9. **Read aloud and audit per item:** inspect the question, choices, hints and explanation in context. Log what changed, stable ID, source, why, and any editorial caveats. Verify sources, syntax, display, navigation, modes and real devices separately; don't confuse automated QA with human tone approval.

### Example (Peter, Luke 5:8–11)
**Too formal:** “Jesus did not send Peter away. He reassured him and spoke of the meaningful work ahead of him.”

**Natural:** “But look at how Jesus responded! He didn't turn Peter away. He reassured him and gave him meaningful work to do.”

**Why:** The human warmth is in the *contrast*, not in extra enthusiasm pasted onto every line. The Bible reports Jesus' response; our wording mustn't invent Peter's inner monologue as fact.

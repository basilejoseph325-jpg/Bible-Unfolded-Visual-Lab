## 1. Purpose and governing principle
Create a *fun, cinematic, accurate, meaningful* Bible game. Never generate filler puzzles, trivial recall, absurd multiple-choice distractors, duplicated discoveries or vague hints just to fill a quota. **Source research comes before the question or puzzle**, not afterward to justify a weak idea. Preserve all previously approved behavior unless the user explicitly changes it.

## 2. Mandatory research and educational content pipeline — James addendum incorporated
1. **Research first from primary approved materials:** the 2013 *New World Translation* on JW.org/JW Library, JW.org Study Bible/Bible-book material, *Insight on the Scriptures*, Watchtower Study Edition and other Watchtower articles, Congregation Bible Study material, JW historical publications, official JW organizational history and other relevant JW.org/JW Library publications. Select the appropriate real source for the account.
2. **Check the actual passage and context.** Record Bible book, exact chapter/verse range, appropriate official JW.org URL, supporting publication and whether a statement is explicit Scripture, commentary, historical understanding, or clearly identified inference. Distinguish older beliefs from current understanding; never attribute undocumented motives or invented feelings to Bible characters.
3. **Pick meaningful discoveries:** key decisions, reasons, motives when attested, alternatives, stakes, consequences, chronology, promises, faith, principles, and interesting historical/linguistic context. Ask *why it matters* and what the player learns. No filler taps, self-answering questions, repetitive “what happened first?” or trivial name recall masquerading as challenge.
4. **Write original, natural, precise questions and word clues**, not long copied passages. Use one unambiguous correct answer grounded in the source. Where more than one reasonable answer applies, rewrite rather than arbitrarily mark one wrong.
5. **Make challenges genuinely challenging at the selected level.** Plausible distractors must come from nearby verses, similar Bible narratives, overlapping characters, reasonable chronological confusion or plausible interpretations. Avoid obviously ridiculous distractors. True Expert questions should require source understanding, cross-text comparison or chronology—not just longer sentences.
6. **Hints must educate without handing over the solution.** Offer meaningful, progressively more useful clues: context/interpretation first, specific evidence second, stronger assistance later. Do not repeat clues, give word length as a paid hint, reveal the target word inadvertently, or charge twice for a single action. Clear distinction between free progressive clues, token-paid letter reveals, and Point-cost full solution.
7. **Always teach after the answer.** Give a brief, genuinely illuminating explanation drawn from checked sources; distinguish explicit Bible words from additional commentary. Provide exact scripture reference(s), and a JW.org link. Bible/book icon first opens an X-close passage-reference modal, and the user chooses to open JW.org. In Trivia, scripture link is above answer options and must not be duplicated below.
8. **Audit duplicates, coverage and difficulty.** Compare against all existing topic/puzzle banks, preserve IDs and progress. Aim for satisfying 10–15 question sessions where enough excellent eligible questions exist; do not pad a short bank. Chapter/session size never dictates how many important insights an account truly supports.
9. **Respect personal conscience and honest uncertainty.** Do not treat reasonable personal decisions as morally wrong; avoid prescriptive scenarios and claims unsupported by the text.
10. **Editorial acceptance test:** Would a knowledgeable tester like James genuinely have to think and learn something useful? Would the challenge be enjoyable as a *game*? If not, research and rewrite it before integration. Story Adventure must remain actual playable, cinematic interaction—not disguised trivia. **Also pass the mandatory friend-to-friend wording process in §2A before integrating ANY future educational content.**

### The voice we are building
The player should feel that **a warm, knowledgeable, caring friend is beside them, explaining something fascinating about the Bible in ordinary spoken language**. Clear, intelligent, accurate and rewarding; not childish, overly familiar, artificially excited, preachy, robotic, academic, cryptic or condescending. A thoughtful newcomer should understand the point **on the first reading**, without having to reread a sentence four times. Keep complexity in the *insight* or *challenge*, never in the wording.

- Prefer direct everyday words, natural sentence lengths, clear people and actions, and a gentle, conversational flow. Use a simple explanation of an unfamiliar title or historical detail the first time it appears. Avoid abstract nouns and passive constructions when a person doing something would be clearer.
- Be precise without sounding like a textbook. Don't write "the account underscores," "the sequence emphasizes," "this serves to illustrate," "the passage distinguishes," "that qualification prevents the inference," "it identifies the distinction," or "the foregoing demonstrates" when simpler human words would explain the same thing. Such wording is a **review flag**, not a banned word list: judge the complete thought, not keywords alone.
- Don't sound like a template. Not every scroll should begin "Think about it," "What's interesting is," or "This teaches us." Vary openings naturally and center the specific event. Avoid repeated filler lessons, forced emotion, invented dialogue, manufactured motivations or unfounded statements about Jehovah's feelings.
- Write only what helps. A short, vivid, accurate 40–70-word scroll is often better than a padded 100-word one; do **not** impose a mechanical length limit. When a genuine biblical distinction needs more explanation, make it clear and worthwhile, not just longer. Meaningful facts stay; tangents and unnecessary scholarly caveats go. Don't simplify away important nuance or turn uncertainty into certainty.
- Human warmth does not mean loose accuracy. Cite/ground the exact NWT/JW.org source and distinguish the biblical statement from an interpretation, reasonable inference or historical comment. Avoid pretending an implication is directly stated in a verse.

### Mandatory BEFORE-INTEGRATION content workflow — complete for each new or revised item
1. **Start with the Bible and purpose.** Read the NWT passage in context and relevant current JW.org/Insight/Watchtower material. Record the exact source and decide what truly interesting idea the player should learn. Do not invent an answer first and hunt for justification afterward.
2. **Write the question conversationally.** One natural, understandable thought; no convoluted setup, ambiguous names, unnatural jargon, misleading assumptions, or confusing double negatives. An expert question can require difficult thinking while still sounding effortless to read.
3. **Prove and test the answer.** Examine the question AND every answer option against the source. Exactly one option must be correct, and every plausible incorrect option must be demonstrably wrong. Verify the stored correct index, names, places and chronology, not just spelling or JSON. **Never preserve an incorrect answer key merely because a technical integrity test says the index did not change.** Any necessary factual fix must be documented separately from wording edits.
4. **Make distractors believable and balanced.** Parallel grammatical structure, comparable detail, meaningful alternatives; no giveaway created by a longer correct option or obviously silly choices. Confirm the claimed difficulty reflects source knowledge, not confusing prose.
5. **Write and independently test the free hint.** Imagine a player has only the question, answer choices and hint—not the Bible or an outside link. Does the hint provide concrete, truthful help that actually narrows the choice or reasoning? Does it avoid simply repeating the question or giving away the answer? If not, rewrite it. **A source reference alone is NOT a helpful clue.**
6. **Test the stronger/paid help separately.** It must materially help more than the free hint and agree with all choices. Preserve the authorized mode-specific mechanic, price and scoring. For standard Trivia, an approved 15-Insight-Token wrong-option elimination is **not** a newly written prose hint; verify the eliminated choice is wrong. Do not silently redesign paid help.
7. **Write the post-answer scroll with the real question and correct answer visible.** Start by making the answer understandable in plain language (define an unfamiliar term immediately). Then add just the relevant story, reason, consequence or memorable insight that makes the answer worthwhile. Every sentence should answer "How does this help the player understand?". Avoid parroting the correct option or introducing a confusing side issue in the closing sentence.
8. **Perform the first-read / read-aloud test.** Read question, options, free hint, paid aid label and scroll the way a real friend would say them out loud. Flag stiffness, awkward pacing, long chained clauses, unclear pronouns, overly formal vocabulary, confusing jump cuts, repeated points, and anything requiring an unnecessary second reading. Rewrite until it sounds natural without losing biblical accuracy. A reader should be able to restate the answer and the new insight in one simple sentence afterward.
9. **Do a separate editorial signoff.** Check factual accuracy, one-right-answer logic, educational value, challenge level, useful hints, and **human readability as its own pass/fail requirement**. The original author may draft and revise; a separate deliberate check or reviewer should challenge the final copy. Do not claim independent review when none occurred. A script or AI-assisted bulk rewrite alone cannot certify naturalness.
10. **Keep item-level evidence.** Record topic, stable item ID, source verse/URL, question, correct answer/index, former wording, revised wording, reason for change, free-hint usefulness, paid-hint behavior, source verification, readability outcome (**rewritten / retained / needs work**) and reviewer/date. A blanket "all reviewed" note is unacceptable without an item-by-item record. Retain excellent existing text untouched instead of rewriting it solely to inflate edit counts.
11. **Gate integration and publication.** A new question or updated scroll cannot be marked editorially complete or merged for release while any of the above substantive checks fail or are missing. Run structural/runtime tests **after** editorial signoff, and separately test actual rendered scrolls, mobile wrapping, answer/hint behavior and save-progress behavior. JavaScript syntax checks and original-field preservation are not readability, biblical fact, browser or real-phone certification. Label every coverage and QA claim honestly.

### Friendly writing model — concrete example
**Robotic:** "The title expresses God's being with his people through his Son. The identity of an earlier Immanuel remains uncertain."

**Friend-to-friend:** "Immanuel means ‘With Us Is God.’ Long before Jesus was born, Isaiah used that name in a prophecy. Matthew later showed how it applied to Jesus. By sending his Son, Jehovah showed that he was still with his people and keeping his promises."

The second version defines the term right away, explains the connection naturally, and gives the player a point to remember. Verify every formulation against the actual passage and question; an example does not replace source review. The goal is **not** to add a chatty introductory phrase to old academic prose. Rewrite the *thinking and structure* when necessary.
